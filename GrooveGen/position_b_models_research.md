# Position B: Real-Time, Tempo-Locked, Controllable Music Generation — Model Landscape (2024–2026)

**Prepared for: GrooveGen (Position B) — closed-loop biofeedback music system**
**Baseline being replaced:** `facebook/musicgen-small`, offline, ~30s chunks, no tempo/beat conditioning, wanders in tempo, no continuation.

This report evaluates each candidate model/technique against five capabilities required for Position B:
1. Streaming/real-time generation speed and latency
2. Explicit tempo/beat/downbeat conditioning
3. Controllability of style/genre/density/instrumentation via continuous signals (not just text)
4. Fine-tuning feasibility + licensing for research/publication
5. Continuation/infilling for seamless splicing

## Detailed findings

### 1. MusicGen family (Meta) — the current baseline

MusicGen is a single-stage autoregressive transformer operating on EnCodec tokens (50 Hz, 4 codebooks), generating ~50 AR steps per second of audio ([AudioCraft MusicGen docs](https://github.com/facebookresearch/audiocraft/blob/main/docs/MUSICGEN.md)). On consumer/prosumer GPUs, real-world generation speed is well below real-time for anything but the smallest model: community benchmarks report MusicGen-small at roughly 2–5x real-time *only* on strong GPUs, with 30-second clips taking ~10s on GPU vs. minutes on CPU ([musicgen-minimal PyPI benchmarks](https://pypi.org/project/musicgen-minimal/1.1.2)), while MusicGen-large runs at roughly 0.3x real-time on an L4-class GPU ([MysticStage adaptive soundtrack analysis](https://mst-sg.com/generative-music-scores-adaptive-soundtracks-player-mood/)). A token-level streaming demo exists (play audio in ~5s chunks as tokens are generated) which reduces *perceived* latency but does not fix the fundamental generation-speed ceiling or provide tempo control ([HF streaming MusicGen space](https://huggingface.co/spaces/sanchit-gandhi/musicgen-streaming)).

Crucially, **MusicGen has no explicit BPM/tempo/beat conditioning mechanism** — its only non-text conditioning is melody/chroma extracted from a reference audio clip ([MusicGen Melody docs](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/blob/main/docs/source/en/model_doc/musicgen_melody.md)), which constrains pitch contour, not rhythm or tempo grid. Community issue trackers confirm the lack of a reliable on-beat/downbeat generation mechanism ([audiocraft issue #414 "On-beat Generation from MusicGen"](https://github.com/facebookresearch/audiocraft/issues/414)).

**License**: weights are CC-BY-NC 4.0 — non-commercial only ([HF license discussion](https://huggingface.co/spaces/facebook/MusicGen/discussions/8), [LICENSE_weights](https://huggingface.co/spaces/facebook/MusicGen/blob/948eca59d41e9edf5065d673bb62aca77b8d6b71/LICENSE_weights)) — fine for a PhD research context, not for a commercialized product.

**Verdict**: MusicGen (and its melody/stereo variants) remains fundamentally the wrong architecture class for Position B: no tempo lock, chunk-capped generation, and sub-real-time throughput on most consumer hardware. It is a good *conditioning target* for add-on techniques (MusiConGen, JASCO's architecture lineage) but not viable as-is.

### 2. MAGNeT (Meta) — non-autoregressive, faster, but no tempo fix

MAGNeT is a single-stage, non-autoregressive masked-generative transformer over EnCodec tokens, achieving audio quality comparable to MusicGen while being **~7x faster** due to parallel decoding across masked spans ([arXiv:2401.04577](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04577v2), [AudioCraft MAGNeT docs](https://facebookresearch.github.io/audiocraft/docs/MAGNET.html)). Reported latency is as low as **600ms for a single sample at small batch size** — about 10x faster than the autoregressive baseline — making it genuinely interesting for interactive applications ([Meta FAIR LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/aiatmeta_as-part-of-our-audiocraft-work-researchers-activity-7163591049146290176-zpo6)). A hybrid AR/NAR mode generates the first few seconds autoregressively (for a strong "seed") then completes the rest in parallel.

However, MAGNeT has **no built-in tempo or beat conditioning**, and being non-autoregressive complicates true streaming continuation — the mask-and-refine inference process is not naturally causal/chunk-continuable the way an AR model or the Magenta RT block-AR architecture is. Officially released checkpoints only support 10s/30s fixed-length generation (no long-form streaming) ([facebook/magnet-medium-30secs model card](https://huggingface.co/facebook/magnet-medium-30secs)).

**License**: code MIT, weights CC-BY-NC 4.0 — same non-commercial constraint as MusicGen.

**Verdict**: MAGNeT is a legitimate *speed* upgrade over MusicGen but does not solve tempo-lock or seamless splicing; it would need the same kind of rhythm-conditioning bolt-on that MusiConGen/JASCO provide for MusicGen, and no such MAGNeT-specific variant is documented as released.

### 3. JASCO (Meta) — best released controllability, capped clip length

JASCO ("Joint Audio and Symbolic Conditioning") is a flow-matching (continuous, not discrete-token) text-to-music model that jointly accepts **global text description** plus **local temporal controls**: chords (symbolic), drums (audio-based, source-separated), and melody (symbolic, multi-F0), via information-bottleneck + temporal-blurring conditioning layers ([arXiv:2406.10970](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10970), [AudioCraft JASCO docs](https://github.com/facebookresearch/audiocraft/blob/main/docs/JASCO.md)). This is architecturally the most relevant released technique for GrooveGen's goal of conditioning on more than just text.

Released checkpoints: `facebook/jasco-chords-drums-400M`, `-1B`, and `-chords-drums-melody-400M/1B` variants, **each generating 10-second samples** ([HF model card](https://huggingface.co/facebook/jasco-chords-drums-1B/resolve/main/README.md?download=true)). Inference uses an ODE/Euler solver (default 10 steps) — this is markedly cheaper per-sample than a full diffusion U-Net with 100+ steps, but **no public real-time-factor or GPU-latency benchmark was found** in official docs or third-party writeups; do not assume real-time without direct testing.

Tempo is *not* a literal numeric parameter — rhythm control comes via the audio-derived drum-stem conditioning (extract a drum pattern from a reference track) rather than a "set BPM=110" API. This is more precise than MusicGen's melody conditioning but still indirect.

**License**: inference code MIT, pretrained weights **CC-BY-NC 4.0** ([Music Business Worldwide coverage](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/metas-new-generative-ai-model-can-take-chords-or-beats-and-turn-them-into-songs/), [HF model card](https://huggingface.co/facebook/jasco-chords-drums-1B/resolve/main/README.md?download=true)) — fine for academic publication, not commercial.

**Verdict**: JASCO is the **strongest released open technique for multi-signal controllability** (chords + drums + melody + text simultaneously), but (a) it is capped at 10-second clips with no continuation mechanism demonstrated, (b) no confirmed real-time speed, and (c) tempo is implicit rather than an explicit numeric control. It would need engineering work (chaining 10s segments, adding an explicit BPM-to-drum-pattern generator) to fit GrooveGen.

### 4. Music ControlNet — most precise beat/tempo curve control found in literature

Music ControlNet (CMU + Adobe Research) adapts the image-domain ControlNet method to a diffusion model operating on Mel spectrograms, adding **three composable, time-varying controls**: melody (chroma), dynamics (loudness envelope), and **rhythm** — explicitly modeled as frame-wise beat/downbeat probability curves from an RNN-based beat detector ([arXiv:2311.07069](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.07069)). Critically, the paper states rhythm control **can be created at inference time by time-stretching beat/downbeat probability curves extracted from existing songs to match a desired tempo** — this is the most literal "give me an exact BPM/beat grid" mechanism found among all reviewed models.

The model is dramatically smaller than MusicGen (41M vs. 1.5B parameters) and trained on 11x less data, yet the paper reports it is **49% more faithful to input melody** than MusicGen's melody conditioning ([arXiv:2311.07069](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.07069)). Any subset of controls can be partially specified in time, letting the model "improvise" the rest — directly useful for a biofeedback system that wants to lock rhythm/tempo but leave melodic content free.

**Caveats (be critical here)**: (1) No public real-time-factor benchmark was found; being a diffusion model on spectrograms with a vocoder stage adds decode latency. (2) It is unclear whether official pretrained weights are publicly released for reuse (the GitHub/demo pages found are primarily research artifacts and demo audio — verify current repo state before committing engineering time). (3) A DiT-based extension applying the same ControlNet idea to Stable Audio's architecture exists (["Editing Music with Melody and Text," 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.05151v1)), suggesting the technique is portable but each instantiation needs re-verification of release status.

**Verdict**: Conceptually the best match for "exact beat grid" control of any model reviewed. High-priority candidate for either (a) locating/reproducing the released weights, or (b) reimplementing the ControlNet-on-spectrogram or ControlNet-on-DiT approach as a research contribution in its own right — this could BE a novel contribution of the GrooveGen PhD project.

### 5. DITTO / DITTO-2 — training-free control, but added inference cost

DITTO (Diffusion Inference-Time T-Optimization) is a **training-free** framework: instead of fine-tuning, it optimizes the *initial noise latents* of a pretrained text-to-music diffusion model against an arbitrary differentiable feature-matching loss (e.g., matching a target intensity curve, melody, or musical structure) ([arXiv:2401.12179](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.12179v2)). It supports inpainting, outpainting, and looping — all directly relevant to seamless splicing — and the paper reports it **outperforms Music ControlNet on melody/intensity control with zero supervised training**.

The catch: **the original DITTO is >10x slower than real-time**, because gradient-based optimization through the diffusion sampling process is expensive ([arXiv:2405.20289](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20289)). DITTO-2 fixes this by (1) distilling the diffusion model for few-step sampling via consistency/consistency-trajectory distillation, (2) doing the inference-time optimization on the cheap distilled model as a surrogate, then (3) running one final multi-step high-quality decode — yielding a **10-20x speedup**, which can plausibly bring total latency into a near-real-time or better-than-real-time regime on an A100-class GPU, while *improving* control adherence over the non-distilled version ([arXiv:2405.20289](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20289)).

**Verdict**: DITTO-2 is the most promising **technique** (not a turnkey model) for adding precise, flexible, non-text control (including tempo-adjacent feature curves) on top of an existing fast diffusion backbone, without fine-tuning. Its main limitation for GrooveGen: it is a per-clip optimization procedure, so integrating it into a truly continuous, always-on live stream (as opposed to controlled regeneration of short segments) requires system-level engineering; it is not itself a streaming architecture like Magenta/Lyria RT.

### 6. Stable Audio family (Stability AI)

- **Stable Audio Open 1.0**: DiT-based latent diffusion, up to 47s stereo @ 44.1kHz, ~1.1B params, runs on consumer GPUs, finetunable (Stability recommends A6000-class for training) ([arXiv:2407.14358](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.14358v1), [Stability AI research paper announcement](https://stability.ai/news-updates/stable-audio-open-research-paper)). No native BPM parameter — users write tempo into the text prompt (e.g., `"128 BPM tech house drum loop"`) with no guarantee of grid accuracy ([HF model card generation example](https://huggingface.co/stabilityai/stable-audio-open-1.0)). License: Stability AI Community License — free for research and for commercial use under $1M revenue.

- **Stable Audio Open Small**: A 341M-parameter distillation/optimization of Stable Audio Open, built with Arm for **on-device** generation — **11 seconds of audio in under 8 seconds on a smartphone CPU**, and remarkably **~75ms to generate 12 seconds of audio on an H100 GPU** ([Stability AI announcement](https://stability.ai/news-updates/stability-ai-and-arm-release-stable-audio-open-small-enabling-real-world-deployment-for-on-device-audio-control), [Arm technical blog](https://developer.arm.com/community/arm-community-blogs/b/ai-blog/posts/audio-generation-arm-cpus-stable-audio-open-small-kleidiai)). This is genuinely real-time-or-faster on GPU. However, it is explicitly optimized for **short sound effects, drum loops, and instrument riffs**, not sustained multi-minute tempo-locked musical beds, and does not document tempo/BPM conditioning. License: Stability AI Community License, Apache 2.0 for the Arm-side glue code.

- **Stable Audio 2.0 / 3.0**: 2.0 adds audio-to-audio style transfer and up to 3-minute structured tracks via a DiT operating on a heavily compressed latent (21.5 Hz latent rate) ([Stability AI announcement](https://stability.ai/news-updates/stable-audio-2-0), [demo page](https://stability-ai.github.io/stable-audio-2-demo/)); 2.0 itself is API/product-only (not open-weights). **Stable Audio 3.0** introduces open-weight **Small** and **Medium** variants alongside a commercial "Large" ([Stability AI Stable Audio 3.0 page](https://stability.ai/stable-audio)) — this is a notable 2025/2026-era development worth prototyping against, though at time of writing no independent real-time-factor benchmarks for 3.0 Medium/Small were found; verify current status directly before relying on it.

**Verdict**: Stable Audio Open Small is the fastest **released, runnable, open** model found for short-clip generation, but is tuned for SFX/loops, not tempo-locked musical phrases with sustained control. Stable Audio Open (full) and 3.0 Medium offer better musicality with acceptable finetuning cost, but no exact-BPM guarantee.

### 7. MusicLM / SoundStorm (Google) — landmark techniques, not directly usable

MusicLM was Google's original hierarchical semantic+acoustic token text-to-music model; it was never released with public weights and is functionally superseded by the Magenta RT / Lyria RT lineage below, which explicitly "adapt the MusicLM architecture." It offers no tempo control and no path to reproducible open use.

SoundStorm is the more important legacy piece: a non-autoregressive, bidirectional-attention "MaskGIT for audio" model that generates **30 seconds of audio in 0.5 seconds on a TPU-v4** — two orders of magnitude faster than AudioLM's autoregressive decoder ([arXiv:2305.09636](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.09636), [Google Research blog](https://research.google/blog/soundstorm-efficient-parallel-audio-generation/)). This is conceptually the ancestor of MAGNeT's approach. **Critically, Google never released official SoundStorm weights or code** — only unofficial, lower-fidelity community reproductions exist ([community reimplementation](https://github.com/yangdongchao/SoundStorm)). It is not directly usable for GrooveGen; its main value is as a design reference for "how to make masked non-AR audio generation fast."

### 8. Magenta RealTime & Lyria RealTime (Google DeepMind) — the architecturally closest match

This pairing is the single most important finding of this research pass, and was **not on the original candidate list** — flagging it as the most relevant addition.

Both models implement **block-autoregressive live music generation**: the model outputs sequential 2-second audio chunks, each conditioned on (a) the previous ~10 seconds of its own output and (b) a live-updatable style embedding (a weighted mix of text/audio prompt embeddings) ([Magenta RT announcement](https://magenta.withgoogle.com/magenta-realtime), [Lyria RT announcement](https://magenta.withgoogle.com/lyria-realtime), [Live Music Models paper, arXiv:2508.04651](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.04651.pdf)). This is architecturally almost exactly the closed-loop biofeedback use case: continuous generation + live steerable control + causal streaming.

- **Magenta RT** (open-weights): 800M-parameter autoregressive transformer, SpectroStream 48kHz stereo codec, trained on ~190k hours mostly-instrumental stock music. On a free Colab TPU v2-8, it generates 2s of audio in 1.25s (**real-time factor 1.6**) ([Magenta RT page](https://magenta.withgoogle.com/magenta-realtime)). Weights are on Hugging Face/GCS under a permissive license with "additional bespoke terms" — read those terms carefully before committing to it for a dissertation deliverable. Local on-device inference is announced as "coming soon," not yet shipped at time of research. It does not expose a hard numeric BPM control in the public-facing description (tempo/rhythm is implicit in the style embedding and training data).

- **Lyria RealTime** (closed, API-only via Gemini API `lyria-realtime-exp`): shares the same core architecture but exposes a **materially richer control surface**, including an **explicit `bpm` parameter (60–200 range)**, `density`, `brightness`, `scale` (key/mode), and stem-muting controls (`mute_bass`, `mute_drums`, `only_bass_and_drums`) ([Google AI live_music API docs](https://ai.google.dev/api/live_music.md.txt), [Gemini API real-time music generation guide](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/realtime-music-generation)). Control latency is documented at **maximum 2 seconds** between a control change and its audible effect. Note the important operational detail: **changing BPM or scale requires calling `reset_context()`**, which causes a hard audio discontinuity — i.e., Lyria RT can hold a rock-solid tempo *within* a session, but changing tempo is not seamless; this is directly relevant to how GrooveGen would need to handle biofeedback-driven tempo changes.

The underlying research paper ("Live Music Models," [arXiv:2508.04651](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.04651.pdf)) explicitly documents **temporal/BPM conditioning training** using an off-the-shelf beat-prediction model to annotate training data with BPM labels, and discusses using audio-injection / "looper pedal"-like techniques to keep a live performer's tempo locked to the model's output — this is unusually close to the exact GrooveGen closed-loop design problem, and is worth reading in full as directly relevant prior art.

**Key limitations documented by Google itself**: (1) 2-second control latency means style/parameter changes are not instantaneous; (2) the 10-second audio context window means the model cannot "remember" or directly reference anything generated more than 10 seconds ago, so it cannot autonomously build long-term song structure — every "structure" has to come from the operator's live steering; (3) Lyria RT is API-only — no weights, no self-hosting, no true fine-tuning, which is a reproducibility and long-term-access risk for a PhD project that needs a citable, durable pipeline.

**Verdict**: **Architecturally, this is the best-fit existing system for Position B's real-time/tempo-locked/controllable requirements** — Lyria RT in particular has literally the numeric BPM slider GrooveGen wants. But it is a closed Google API (licensing/reproducibility risk for research publication and long-term system ownership), and Magenta RT (the open cousin) does not yet expose the same explicit BPM control or on-device path. Recommend prototyping against Lyria RT's API to validate the *interaction design* for GrooveGen, while treating Magenta RT (and its future on-device release) as the long-term open target — and/or using Lyria RT's published architecture description as a blueprint to inform a from-scratch or fine-tuned open implementation.

### 9. YuE — irrelevant for this use case, but worth ruling out explicitly

YuE (HKUST/M-A-P) is a LLaMA2-architecture autoregressive model for **full-song, lyrics-to-song generation** up to 5 minutes, comparable in ambition to Suno/Udio ([arXiv:2503.08638](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.08638v1), [YuE GitHub](https://github.com/multimodal-art-projection/YuE)). It is impressively open (Apache 2.0, including generated outputs) but **fundamentally the wrong shape of model**: inference is ~150s for 30s of audio on an H800 GPU, or ~360s for 30s on an RTX 4090 ([community benchmark summary](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/1889100803618873719)) — i.e., roughly **12x slower than real-time even on a high-end consumer card**, and the full pipeline needs ~80GB VRAM ([Heise coverage](https://www.heise.de/en/news/Open-source-music-generator-YuE-creates-songs-offline-from-song-lyrics-10267391.html)). No tempo/BPM conditioning is exposed. Not viable for Position B.

### 10. Riffusion — historically notable, technically obsolete

Riffusion fine-tunes Stable Diffusion 1.5 to generate spectrogram *images* from text, then converts them to audio ([Riffusion GitHub](https://github.com/riffusion/riffusion-hobby), [HF model card](https://huggingface.co/riffusion/riffusion-model-v1)). It pioneered the idea of real-time-ish generative music via image-diffusion tricks (latent-space interpolation between prompts for pseudo-continuity) and is fully MIT-licensed, but audio quality and tempo coherence are far below 2024–2026 purpose-built audio models. Useful as a historical reference for "diffusion in spectrogram space," and its interpolation approach is conceptually a precursor to the smoother approaches in Music ControlNet/DITTO, but not a serious 2026 candidate.

### 11. Suno / Udio — closed, tempo-drift-prone, and legally entangled

Both are commercial, closed, cloud-only full-song generators. Even setting aside architecture, two practical problems disqualify them for GrooveGen: (1) **Documented tempo drift** — users report tracks specified at, e.g., 78 BPM drifting to 93 BPM, or fluctuating across a session even when a fixed BPM tag is set; fixing this currently requires a premium "Studio" tier with manual tempo-lock editing, not an API-level guarantee ([r/SunoAI tempo drift thread, Nov 2025](https://www.reddit.com/r/SunoAI/comments/1ovbcfu/tempo_drift/)). (2) **Ongoing litigation and licensing complexity** — RIAA lawsuits against both Suno and Udio (filed June 2024) have led to partial settlements (UMG–Udio, Oct 2025; WMG–Udio and WMG–Suno, Nov 2025) that impose per-generation royalties and content-ID obligations on the platforms, while Suno continues to litigate rather than settle ([AI Vortex legal timeline](https://www.aivortex.io/legal/ai-case-law/suno-udio-music-ai/), [Forbes coverage](https://www.forbes.com/sites/virginieberger/2025/12/18/launch-train-settle-how-suno-and-udios-licensing-deals-made-copyright-infringement-profitable/)). For a PhD publication that needs a stable, ethically clean, reproducible pipeline, building on Suno/Udio is inadvisable regardless of technical fit.

### 12. ACE-Step / ACE-Step 1.5 — fast, open, explicit BPM metadata; not yet a streaming instrument

ACE-Step is a 2025-era open-source "foundation model for music" combining a compressed DCAE audio autoencoder with a DiT-style transformer, plus (in v1.5) a Qwen-based LLM "Composer Agent" front-end that plans structured metadata — **explicitly including BPM, key/scale, time signature, and duration** — before generation ([ACE-Step 1.5 GitHub](https://github.com/ace-step/ACE-Step-1.5), [ACE-Step project page](https://ace-step.github.io/), [ACE-Step 1.5 arXiv](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2602.00744v2.pdf)). Reported speed is extraordinary for a batch generator: **v1 generates up to 4 minutes of music in ~20s on an A100 (15x faster than real time)**; **v1.5 generates a full song in under 2 seconds on an A100 and under 10 seconds on an RTX 3090**, and can run with as little as 4GB VRAM ([awesome-ace-step repo](https://github.com/ace-step/awesome-ace-step)).

A separate, directly relevant 2026 arXiv paper ("Real-Time Interactive Music Generation via Data-Free Streaming Consistency Distillation," [arXiv:2606.24307](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.24307v1)) explicitly builds a **streaming, chunk-wise, one-step-per-chunk distillation** on top of an ACE-Step-XL-Turbo teacher model, reporting a startup latency of **0.086s** and real-time factor of **0.009** (i.e., ~100x faster than real time) on an H200 GPU — this is arguably the most advanced "streaming distillation for controllable live music" result found in this research pass, and is very close in spirit to what GrooveGen needs (chunk-wise cached context, phrase-level control latency ~0.5–2s, asynchronous decoding). It is very new (2026) and its code/model release status should be checked directly before relying on it, but it is a strong signal that the field is actively solving exactly this problem in 2025–2026.

**Verdict**: ACE-Step's explicit BPM/metadata control plus open weights plus consumer-GPU speed make it a strong open candidate — but the *base* ACE-Step models are batch ("generate the whole song, then play it") rather than a persistent live-streaming instrument like Magenta/Lyria RT. The distillation follow-up work shows the streaming version is achievable and is worth tracking closely or replicating as part of GrooveGen's own contribution.

### 13. MusiConGen — the most directly actionable near-term upgrade to the current pipeline

MusiConGen (AMAAI-Lab / Yating Music, ISMIR 2024) fine-tunes MusicGen-Melody (1.5B) to add **rhythm and chord conditioning** without requiring a reference audio clip at inference time, using a novel "jump fine-tuning" mechanism specifically designed to be trainable on **consumer-grade GPUs (4× RTX-3090 used in the paper)** ([arXiv:2407.15060](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.15060v1), [MusiConGen GitHub](https://github.com/YatingMusic/MusiConGen), [demo page](https://musicongen.github.io/musicongen_demo/)). Rhythm conditioning is derived from automatically-extracted beat/downbeat detection and can be time-stretched to a target tempo (same principle as Music ControlNet's rhythm curves); chords are extracted via the BTC-ISMIR2019 chord-recognition model.

Documented hardware requirements are refreshingly concrete: **inference needs >12GB VRAM** (single consumer GPU feasible — an RTX 4090's 24GB comfortably covers this), and **fine-tuning needs >24GB VRAM**, with both single-GPU and 4-GPU training configurations documented in the repo ([MusiConGen GitHub](https://github.com/YatingMusic/MusiConGen)). License is **MIT for the new code**, inheriting MusicGen's CC-BY-NC 4.0 for the underlying pretrained weights — i.e., fine for non-commercial PhD research and publication.

**Limitations**: it inherits MusicGen's autoregressive decoding profile (same real-time-factor problems as MusicGen — no independent streaming benchmark published), the same ~30-second clip cap, and rhythm control is still "time-stretched beat curve," not a literal single-number BPM API — though this is trivially wrapped (generate a click/beat curve at the target BPM programmatically, feed it in).

**Verdict**: **This is the most concretely actionable, reproducible, and citable near-term upgrade path from GrooveGen's current MusicGen-small baseline.** It directly solves the "no known/fixed downbeat" and "cannot be conditioned on exact BPM" problems documented in the task brief, using open, MIT-licensed code, on hardware GrooveGen likely already has access to (a single RTX 3090/4090-class GPU). It does not solve the streaming/real-time-generation-speed problem or the 30-second chunk cap — those would still need to be solved separately (e.g., by adopting MAGNeT-style non-AR decoding, or migrating the rhythm/chord conditioning idea onto a Magenta-RT-style block-autoregressive backbone as a novel research contribution).

## Cross-cutting technical notes

**On "real-time" claims — be skeptical of headline numbers.** Many "Nx faster than real-time" claims (ACE-Step's 15x, MAGNeT's 7x, SoundStorm's 100x+) are measured on **A100/H100/TPU-v4-class hardware with batch size 1 and no additional control-signal overhead**. None of these numbers directly transfer to "the whole GrooveGen pipeline, including EEG/physio feature extraction, control mapping, model inference, and audio buffering, runs with sub-2-second closed-loop latency on an RTX 4090." Any adopted model needs to be benchmarked end-to-end in the actual target deployment environment, not just cited from a paper's isolated GPU benchmark.

**On tempo "conditioning" vs. tempo "guarantee."** Only two systems reviewed have literal numeric BPM parameters with any documentation of enforcement: **Lyria RealTime** (`bpm: 60–200`, requires context reset to change) and **ACE-Step** (BPM as generation metadata, enforced by training the model to condition on BPM-labeled data). Every other model either has no BPM control, or achieves tempo influence only indirectly (audio-derived rhythm/drum conditioning in JASCO/MusiConGen/Music ControlNet, or loose text hints in MusicGen/Stable Audio). Even commercial systems with tempo controls (Suno) show real documented tempo drift in production. **This confirms the task brief's framing is correct: exact, enforced tempo-lock is still a genuinely open, mostly-unsolved problem** in released open-source music generation as of mid-2026 — Lyria RT (closed) and the ACE-Step-distillation streaming paper (very new, 2026) are the two strongest counterexamples.

**On continuation/splicing.** True chunk-to-chunk seamless continuation (the model conditions each new chunk on its own immediately preceding output) is a defining feature of only the **block-autoregressive live-music family**: Magenta RealTime, Lyria RealTime, and MusicLM (their common ancestor). DITTO/DITTO-2's inpainting/outpainting is the next best mechanism, but works at the level of optimizing/regenerating a segment against a target, not a persistent streaming state. Everything else (MusicGen, MAGNeT, JASCO, Stable Audio, YuE, ACE-Step) is fundamentally clip-based, requiring engineering (crossfading, careful prompt/seed continuity, or DITTO-style feature-matched regeneration) to approximate seamlessness.

**On licensing for a PhD project.** Nearly all Meta (MusicGen, MAGNeT, JASCO) and several Stability AI weights are CC-BY-NC-style — this is **fine for academic research and publication**, which is squarely GrooveGen's context, but would block any future commercialization without renegotiating licenses. ACE-Step, YuE, MusiConGen's new code, and Riffusion are meaningfully more permissive (Apache 2.0/MIT), which is worth weighting if GrooveGen anticipates any translational/commercial follow-on.

## Bottom line: top 3 most promising paths

**1. Adopt MusiConGen now as an immediate, low-risk upgrade; treat Music ControlNet's rhythm-curve idea as the design pattern to replicate/extend.**
MusiConGen is open (MIT + inherited MusicGen CC-BY-NC), runs on hardware GrooveGen likely already has (>12GB VRAM for inference, >24GB for fine-tuning — an RTX 4090 covers both), and directly solves the "no fixed downbeat / can't condition on exact BPM" complaint by fine-tuning MusicGen to accept extracted, tempo-time-stretched rhythm and chord curves ([arXiv:2407.15060](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.15060v1), [GitHub](https://github.com/YatingMusic/MusiConGen)). Music ControlNet's approach ([arXiv:2311.07069](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.07069)) is the more elegant and precise version of the same idea (explicit beat/downbeat probability curves, partial specification, dynamics control) but its public reusability is less certain — verify weight availability, and if unavailable, consider reimplementing the ControlNet-on-spectrogram (or ControlNet-on-DiT, per the [2024 extension](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.05151v1)) approach as a genuine research contribution. **Tradeoff**: neither model solves the fundamental real-time-generation-speed or 30–47-second clip-cap problem — this path fixes tempo/rhythm controllability first, leaving streaming speed as a second engineering phase (e.g., layering MAGNeT-style non-AR decoding or chunked crossfading on top).

**2. Prototype against Lyria RealTime's API to de-risk the interaction design, while tracking Magenta RealTime (open) as the long-term target architecture.**
The block-autoregressive live-music architecture behind both Magenta RT and Lyria RT ([arXiv:2508.04651](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.04651.pdf)) is the only reviewed system that combines genuine real-time factor >1, ≤2-second control latency, and — in Lyria RT's case — a literal numeric `bpm` parameter plus density/brightness/key controls exposed over a live WebSocket ([Google AI live_music docs](https://ai.google.dev/api/live_music.md.txt)). This is the closest match to GrooveGen's stated goal of a system that "GENERATES novel audio LIVE, conditioned on a target musical state... stays tempo-stable." **Tradeoff**: Lyria RT is closed/API-only (no weights, no self-hosting, no fine-tuning, and a research-use/publication-terms question that needs resolving with Google before building a dissertation around it), and its open cousin Magenta RT does not yet expose the same explicit BPM control or shipped on-device path. Use Lyria RT to validate *what the interaction should feel like* and to benchmark against, but plan for either Magenta RT's eventual on-device release or a from-scratch/fine-tuned reproduction of the block-AR architecture as the actual deliverable.

**3. Track ACE-Step (and its 2026 streaming-distillation follow-up) as the fastest-moving open alternative, and DITTO-2 as the best training-free control technique to layer onto any backbone.**
ACE-Step already ships open weights with explicit BPM/key/time-signature metadata conditioning and startlingly fast batch generation (sub-2-second to ~10-second full songs on A100/RTX 3090) ([GitHub](https://github.com/ace-step/ACE-Step-1.5), [arXiv:2602.00744](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2602.00744v2.pdf)), and a very recent (2026) distillation paper built on an ACE-Step-XL-Turbo teacher demonstrates genuine chunk-wise streaming with sub-100ms startup latency and real-time factors around 0.01 ([arXiv:2606.24307](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.24307v1)) — strong evidence the open-source community is actively converging on exactly what GrooveGen needs. In parallel, DITTO-2's training-free, distilled inference-time optimization ([arXiv:2405.20289](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20289)) is the best available technique for adding arbitrary continuous control signals (including tempo-adjacent feature curves) to *any* pretrained diffusion backbone without fine-tuning, at 10–20x lower cost than the original DITTO. **Tradeoff**: both are newer/less battle-tested than Options 1–2 (verify current code/weight release status directly before committing), and neither is yet demonstrated as a complete, published, peer-reviewed closed-loop biofeedback system — meaning GrooveGen adopting/extending them would be closer to novel research than integration work, which may be exactly the right positioning for a PhD contribution.
