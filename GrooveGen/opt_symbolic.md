# Symbolic / MIDI Generation as an Alternative to MusicGen for GrooveGen Beds

## Verdict (top-line)

**Partial fit — technically the right architecture, but not a drop-in replacement, and the timbral/genre realism gap is real.** Symbolic generation *structurally solves* the percussion-entanglement problem MusicGen created: melody/harmony/bass and drums are separate MIDI tracks by construction, tempo is exact and the bar grid is trivially lockable, and per-bar drum density is a first-class, literal control variable rather than a prompt-engineering guess. This directly satisfies GrooveGen's core requirement in a way MusicGen structurally cannot. The cost is that no open, Colab-T4-runnable symbolic model produces genre-authentic *harmonic vocabulary* (salsa montuno voicings, tumbao bass, clave-locked rhythmic phrasing) out of the box — genre identity in symbolic models comes from training data and control tokens, not from an emergent "sound," so salsa/R&B authenticity will require either a fine-tuned/constrained model or rule-based post-processing (chord-progression templates, montuno voicing scripts). Separately, the **audio realism ceiling of FluidSynth+GM soundfonts is a research-stimulus ceiling, not a produced-track ceiling** — usable for a controlled listening/physio experiment, clearly synthetic if judged as music.

**Recommended concrete pipeline to prototype first:** **MMM (Multi-Track Music Machine)** or the **Anticipatory Music Transformer (AMT, Stanford CRFM, `stanford-crfm/music-medium-800k` on Hugging Face)** for generation — both give genuinely separate, simultaneously-generated tracks (melody/harmony/bass one, drums another) with note-density/instrument conditioning and run as plain PyTorch/HF `transformers` models (no TensorFlow/Magenta dependency headaches) — paired with **FluidSynth + a curated multi-instrument SF2 (e.g., FluidR3_GM or a better "salsa/latin" GM soundfont) for rendering**, plus a light rule-based harmonic layer (hand-written montuno/tumbao MIDI templates or chord-conditioned constrained decoding) layered on top to guarantee genre-correct harmony rather than hoping the transformer infers it. This is the most Colab-T4-friable, dependency-light, and controllable of the options surveyed.

**Bottom line on models:** Magenta is the most "batteries included" for multitrack + chord conditioning but is **effectively dead on modern Colab** (hard Python 3.7 ceiling against Colab's Python 3.12 default — [Colab Enterprise runtime docs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/colab/docs/runtimes)). MMM and the Anticipatory Music Transformer are the two credible, currently-installable, genuinely multi-track options; neither has an explicit "genre" conditioning token, so genre fidelity would rely on (a) fine-tuning/prompting with genre-specific MIDI seeds, or (b) hybrid rule-based harmonic scaffolding layered under a generative fill.

## 2. Rendering: MIDI → audio

- **FluidSynth + General MIDI soundfont (e.g., FluidR3_GM)** is the standard, zero-cost, Colab-trivial renderer (`apt install fluidsynth`, `pip install pyFluidSynth`/`midi2audio`). Community consensus (soundfont forums, [Adieu Sounds roundup](https://adieusounds.com/blogs/news/15-free-soundfonts-to-elevate-your-music-production-in-2024)) is that FluidR3_GM is "comprehensive" and serviceable but generic-sounding — sampled at modest velocity layers, with static/looping sustain that reads as MIDI-obviously-synthetic on solo instruments (piano, bass) even if acceptable in a dense mix.
- **Better free SF2/SFZ alternatives exist** for specific instruments (better grand pianos, Rhodes-style electric pianos, fingered bass) via community soundfont libraries (Zanderjaz, ProducersBuzz "900+ free soundfonts," `soundfonts4u` dataset on Hugging Face). Swapping in a higher-quality piano/EP/bass SF2 per genre (rather than default GM patch 0/4/33) measurably improves perceived realism at zero extra latency cost — this is a cheap, high-value swap.
- **Neural MIDI-to-audio renderers** (e.g., **DDSP-Piano**, **MIDI-DDSP**) synthesize meaningfully more realistic timbre than sampled soundfonts for the *instruments they were trained on* — DDSP-Piano's own DAFx evaluation found it beats a SOTA neural piano baseline but still trails physical-modeling synths ([DDSP-Piano, Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/8386706); [Renault thesis](https://theses.hal.science/tel-04732963v1/file/139429_RENAULT_2024_archivage.pdf)). The catch: these are largely **piano-only** (or single-instrument) research systems — there is no off-the-shelf neural renderer covering salsa piano/Rhodes/finger bass/percussion as a set, so adopting one only upgrades one track of the bed, not the whole ensemble, and adds real integration/engineering overhead for a single-instrument gain.
- **Realism ceiling, stated honestly:** FluidSynth+GM is *fine for a controlled research stimulus* — listeners in a physiological/HR experiment are unlikely to be distracted from the manipulation by "not quite a real studio piano," especially if the task is about tempo/density/arousal, not musical fidelity judgment. It is **not** fine as a demo track meant to sound "produced." Best practical answer: FluidSynth + carefully chosen (non-default) SF2 patches per genre, not the raw default GM bank.

## 3. Genre authenticity: what symbolic can and can't capture

- **What it captures well:** Explicit structural facts — chord progressions, note density, instrument roles, bar-aligned phrase structure, tempo, meter — are exactly the dimensions symbolic representations encode natively. A hand-specified or fine-tuned montuno chord-and-rhythm pattern, a I-IV-V pop progression, or a smooth-jazz-adjacent R&B ii-V-I with 7th/9th voicings can all be *encoded losslessly* in MIDI. Per-instrument role separation (piano montuno vs. bass tumbao vs. horns) is trivial to enforce because tracks are already separate.
- **Where it struggles:**
  - **Timbre/production feel**: symbolic + GM synth cannot replicate the saturated, EQ'd, compressed, room-mic'd sound of a real salsa horn section or a warm neo-soul Rhodes — that "feel" is largely a *production/timbre* phenomenon MusicGen (a real-audio model) captures for free and symbolic+softsynth cannot.
  - **Microtiming/groove**: son clave, montuno syncopation, and tumbao "push" depend on sub-beat microtiming and dynamic accenting that generic Transformer sampling tends to quantize away unless the training data and tokenizer explicitly preserve fine timing/velocity (Magenta's GrooVAE was built specifically to add "humanization" back into over-quantized drum performances, underscoring that this is a known, non-trivial gap).
  - **Salsa specifically**: none of the surveyed general-purpose models (trained on Lakh MIDI, which is overwhelmingly rock/pop/classical) have salsa/clave-idiomatic training data; MuPT's genre breakdown of its training corpus lists "World Music (incl. Latin)" at only ~47k songs versus hundreds of thousands of pop/rock, confirming salsa/Latin is a minority class in every general MIDI corpus surveyed. Practically, a generic model prompted for "salsa" will not reliably produce a real montuno/clave/tumbao pattern — this needs either genre-specific fine-tuning data or (more reliably, given time constraints) **hand-authored MIDI templates per genre** (montuno voicing generator, clave-locked tumbao bass line) with the model only filling in variation on top of a scripted skeleton.
  - Pop/R&B/calm are much better served: these are exactly what Lakh-MIDI-trained models have abundant data for, so harmonic idiom (extended chords, smooth voice-leading, ballad melodic contour) should come through far more convincingly than salsa.

## 4. Fit to per-bar control (the core requirement)

This is symbolic's decisive advantage. Drum density per bar is not an emergent, hard-to-steer property of a black-box audio model (as MusicGen's drum-prompt failures showed — [context doc](file:///home/user/workspace/tool_decision_context.md)); it is a **literal, countable, settable property of a MIDI track** — you can directly script "bar 17: exactly 7 note-on events on the drum channel" and render only that track independently of the bed. Because melody/harmony/bass and drums are separate tracks *from generation time*, there is no percussion-bleed-into-harmony problem to begin with, no HPSS/Demucs separation step needed, and no risk of the kind of RMS-ratio failure documented for MusicGen (0.54–0.88 percussion-dominated, worse after prompt rewrites). This maps onto GrooveGen's two-timescale architecture almost exactly: generate the symbolic bed once per segment (slow, fixed), then programmatically write/vary a separate drum MIDI track bar-by-bar at controller-set densities (fast), and mix the two rendered stems. That is a substantially more direct and reproducible implementation of GrooveGen's stated architecture than trying to coax MusicGen into a drumless prompt.

## Overall recommendation

Prototype the pipeline **MMM or Anticipatory Music Transformer (for melody/harmony/bass generation, drums track discarded/replaced) → hand-authored or lightly fine-tuned genre-idiom MIDI scaffolding for salsa specifically → FluidSynth with upgraded (non-default) SF2 patches for rendering → separately-scripted drum MIDI track at controller-set density, rendered independently and mixed in.** This keeps every stated hard requirement (Colab T4, single session, reproducible/lockable grid, instrumental-only, no vocals) satisfiable with commodity, currently-maintained, Python-3.12-friendly tooling, while being honest that genre authenticity — especially salsa — will need scripted/templated harmonic content rather than pure model sampling, and that audio realism will read as "clean research stimulus," not "produced track."
