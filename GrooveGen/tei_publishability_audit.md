## 1. VERDICT: Is this publishable at TEI without human trials?

**Yes — as an Artifact/Method (system) contribution, not as an Empirical contribution.** This is not a hedge; it is how TEI's own review criteria are structured. TEI's 2026/2027 call explicitly separates contribution types and states each "will be peer-reviewed on its own merits": **Artifact** ("research artifacts that advance the state of the art... new sensing techniques or algorithms"), **Method** ("tools, approaches and techniques that enable researchers... new forms of study design"), **Theory**, and **Empirical** (quantitative/qualitative human data) ([TEI 2026 Papers CFP](https://tei.acm.org/2026/participate/papers/)). GrooveGen's cascade controller and generate-ahead architecture is squarely an Artifact/Method claim: "we built a working closed-loop control architecture with these specific properties, demonstrated in simulation and in real audio." That claim needs zero human subjects to be true and defensible.

Precedent exists at TEI for exactly this shape of paper. TEI'26's accepted list includes real-time biosignal-feedback systems and generative-sound systems validated primarily through system demonstration rather than large human trials — e.g. *HyperDance: Real-Time Vibrotactile Stimulation Feedback of Inter-Brain Connectivity in Partner Dance* and *Supporting entrainment using latent space mappings and generative sound feedback during dyadic movement exercises* ([TEI'26 accepted papers](https://tei.acm.org/2026/program/papers/)) — both are systems papers with a technical/architectural core contribution, not primarily large-N efficacy claims. GrooveGen fits this mold better than most: it has a control-theoretic contribution (cascade + generate-ahead) that is legible and evaluable independent of physiological ground truth.

### What claim wording is defensible

| Defensible ("system/method" framing) | Would get desk-rejected ("efficacy" framing) |
|---|---|
| "We present a three-tier cascade controller (genre→tempo→density) for real-time generative music control, and demonstrate in simulation that it converges 6.7× tighter and with 6.4× lower tail variance than a naive parallel baseline." | "Our system reduces user arousal/stress." |
| "We show, via a scripted synthetic HR trace driving real audio regeneration, that the architecture can modulate musical energy in near-real-time with a modeled ~10s generation latency hidden via double-buffering." | "GrooveGen regulates emotion" / "helps users calm down." |
| "Using survey data from 122 participants / 704 tracks, we show empirically that danceability and energy are uncorrelated (r=-0.07) and yield different genre orderings — evidence against a common design assumption in affective-music systems." | "Our genre ordering matches how humans actually experience arousal" (this over-claims what a Spotify-feature correlation shows). |
| "The physiology plant used in simulation is a hand-designed toy model, literature-grounded in sign (higher tempo/density → higher arousal) but not fitted to real physiological data." | Any sentence implying the PD gains, the plant equilibrium bands, or the convergence numbers would transfer quantitatively to a real human loop. |
| "No human closed-loop trials have been run; this paper's contribution is the control architecture and its software/audio instantiation, not a validated emotion-regulation effect." | Anything omitting this sentence, or burying it only in a limitations paragraph after claims that read as if efficacy were shown. |

The single most important sentence to get right in the abstract: state explicitly, in the first paragraph, that validation is via simulation + informal listening + one piece of real (but non-physiological) survey data, and that no human closed-loop or physiological data exists yet. Reviewers who find this omitted or buried will assume it was hidden deliberately, which is worse than the limitation itself.

## 2. Ranked contributions — what's genuinely novel vs. incremental

1. **The cascade control architecture with saturation-gated anti-fight discipline + generate-ahead double-buffering.** *(Most novel.)* This is the real contribution. It's not "we used a PD controller" (PD/PID control of generative media parameters is not new) — it's the specific three-tier hysteresis-gated escalation (density always active → tempo escalates only after `n_bars_saturated_for_tempo` consecutive saturated bars → genre escalates only after tempo *also* saturates) combined with a one-slot commitment buffer that models real generation latency (`hybrid_render` / MusicGen ≈10s per bed). The side-by-side naive-parallel-controller strawman that visibly oscillates while the cascade converges (6.7× tighter final error, 6.4× lower tail variance, [`step_nb13_findings.md`](step_nb13_findings.md)) is a genuinely convincing systems-engineering result, precisely because it demonstrates a *failure mode that would otherwise be invisible* — most closed-loop generative-media demos never show what happens when you *don't* do this. This is TEI-legible: it is a method for coordinating multiple actuators at different timescales in a generative-media control loop, applicable beyond music.

2. **The generate-ahead / commitment-lag model as a first-class, testable object.** Closely tied to #1 but worth separating: explicitly modeling and demonstrating the consequence of ~10s neural-generation latency via a double-buffer (tempo *active* now was committed from state one segment ago) is an engineering contribution specific to closed-loop systems built on top of slow generative models (MusicGen-class). Most "responsive generative music" demos quietly assume instant regeneration; this project's architecture assumes it will be slow and designs around that, which is more honest and more useful to other TEI/NIME-adjacent builders hitting the same wall. Rank this second, not first, only because it is currently proven purely in the simulation's abstract "one segment of lag" — nb15 is what will make it real and audible.

3. **The danceability-vs-energy negative result.** *(The one genuinely real empirical finding.)* N=122 participants, 704 tracks, r=-0.07 between danceability and energy, different genre rank order (R&B>Pop on danceability vs. Pop>R&B on energy) ([`genre_axis_findings.md`](genre_axis_findings.md)). This is real data, clearly analyzed, and it corrects a design assumption (that "danceable" ≈ "arousing") that a naive system-builder would otherwise bake in silently. It is modest in scope — a Spotify-feature correlation on survey data, not a physiology finding — but it is the one place in the whole project where "we checked and we were about to be wrong" is demonstrated with real numbers. Rank it third because its scope is narrow (one design choice, one dataset) relative to the architecture contributions, but flag it as the credibility anchor of the paper: it's proof the team distrusts its own intuitions and checks them, which reviewers reward.

4. **Density-as-monotonic-knob / syncopation-as-non-monotonic-and-therefore-untouched.** This is a real and useful design decision (only touch the lever whose relationship to the target is monotonic across its full range; freeze the non-monotonic one as a fixed "spice" parameter, [`pd_controller.py` docstring](groovegen_project/groovegen/pd_controller.py)) but it is **incremental, not novel** — it is an application of a well-known control-theory principle (don't feedback-control a non-monotonic/inverted-U plant with a linear controller) to a new domain, and the inverted-U claim for syncopation itself is not this project's finding (it cites prior groove literature, e.g. Witek). Present it as a sound design rationale/methods detail, not a headline contribution. Do not rank it alongside #1-#3 in the abstract.

**What is NOT a contribution, and should not be framed as one:** the toy physiology plant itself (`dual_lever_plant_step`, `GENRE_EQUILIBRIUM_BAND`) is engineering scaffolding to make the simulation demo-able, explicitly "illustrative/tuned for a visibly convergent demo, not measured from real listener data" (in-code comment). It is necessary infrastructure, not a result. Do not present its convergence numbers as if they characterize real physiology — they characterize the controller against a plant the authors designed to be controllable.

## 3. The hero artifact: is notebook 15 it, and what must it contain?

**Yes, unambiguously nb15 (end-to-end HR→CascadeController→regenerated audio, hard-cut transitions, one MP4) is the hero artifact — and it should be positioned as the paper's primary figure/video, not a supplementary demo.** Reasoning: TEI is a venue where reviewers and audiences expect to *experience* the interaction claim, not just read a convergence plot. Notebook 13's simulation proves the control math is sound; it cannot, by itself, prove the pipeline survives contact with real audio constraints (real MusicGen latency, real beat-grids, real audible transitions). Notebook 14's crossfade approach already failed the most important test available — the author's own ear — which is the correct outcome to report, not hide: it demonstrates the team holds itself to a real perceptual bar, not just a numerical one. Nb15 (regenerate + downbeat-aligned hard cut) is the fix, and if it works, it is the one artifact that lets a reviewer both **see** the control loop responding to a physiological input and **hear** that the audio result is not degraded/glitchy in the process. No single plot or table can substitute for that.

### Exact must-have spec for notebook 15

**A. The HR curve driving the demo must do three things, not one:**
1. A **down-regulation** segment (elevated HR → falling toward a calm target) — the primary, most TEI-legible use case (this is the "BBCI for emotion regulation" framing the user's whole thesis rests on).
2. An **up-regulation** segment (or a second HR trace) demonstrating the *symmetry* already proven in nb13 (r&b→pop→latin) — this argues the architecture is general, not curve-fit to one direction.
3. At least one **plateau-then-perturbation** (HR settles near target, then a scripted stressor bump) — this is the segment that specifically demonstrates the fast inner density loop rejecting a *disturbance* rather than just tracking a monotonic ramp, which is what most reviewers will assume a PD controller can trivially do and will want to see stress-tested.

Do not use a smooth idealized sine or ramp only — script the HR curve to include at least one abrupt-but-physiologically-plausible feature (e.g., a startle-like bump), because a curve that only ever moves smoothly invites the (fair) criticism that the demo is cherry-picked to make convergence look easy.

**B. What the viewer must be able to SEE (on-screen, synced to the audio's timeline):**
- A live HR/arousal trace (labeled **"SIMULATED"** in the plot title/axis, not just in a caption — this cannot be a caveat that only exists in prose) scrolling in sync with playback.
- The **commanded density and tempo** traces (or bars/stairsteps) directly beneath, same x-axis, so the viewer can visually align "HR moved → lever moved" with a fixed, legible time lag.
- **Genre labeled explicitly** at every transition (text overlay: "genre: r&b → pop"), since genre changes are categorical and easy to miss just by ear if the viewer isn't told to expect a bed change.
- A **`tier_that_acted`** indicator (density/tempo/genre/none) visible per bar or per segment — this is the single most convincing piece of on-screen evidence for the cascade's actual novelty (#1 above): it lets a reviewer see, in real time, that density acts constantly while tempo and genre only kick in when density has saturated. Without this overlay, the cascade's core mechanism is invisible and the video degrades to "music got calmer," which undersells the actual contribution.
- A visible **marker for the generate-ahead lag** — e.g., a small annotation or shaded region showing "committed here, plays N seconds later" at least once — so the commitment-lag contribution (#2) is not only in the code but demonstrably present in the artifact.

**C. What the viewer must HEAR:**
- The **downbeat-aligned hard cut** must be inaudible as a *glitch* (no click, no doubled hit, no dropped beat) even though it is audible as a *transition* (tempo/density/genre change is allowed and expected to be noticeable — the goal is "clean edit," not "imperceptible," which is a lower and more honest bar than crossfading was trying to hit). State this distinction explicitly in the notebook: nb14's crossfade failed because blending two different tempos/grids smeared the beat; nb15's hard cut succeeds by never asking two different tempo grids to occupy the same audio simultaneously.
- At least one A/B moment or clearly labeled "listen for this" instruction embedded in the video (on-screen text or a companion markdown cell) pointing the ear to the exact transition timestamps — do not assume a first-time listener will locate the transitions unaided.

**D. Honest caveats to print inside the notebook itself (not only in the paper):**
- A markdown cell, before the HR curve is introduced, stating verbatim-equivalent to: *"This heart-rate trace is scripted/synthetic, not recorded from a human participant. It is designed to exercise the controller's disturbance-rejection and saturation-escalation behavior, not to represent a validated physiological response to this music."*
- A markdown cell near the plant/mapping code (if any physiology-like mapping is reused from nb13) stating that any arousal→lever mapping is a toy model, literature-grounded in sign only, not fitted to data — reuse the exact caveat language already present in `pd_controller.py`'s `synth_physiology`/`dual_lever_plant_step` docstrings; do not write a weaker version for the notebook.
- A markdown cell disclosing the **genre-order caveat**: Latin sits at N=19 and is "directional, not precise" ([`genre_axis_findings.md` §2](genre_axis_findings.md)); if nb15's HR curve happens to walk into the Latin tier, say so explicitly at that moment, not just once at the top of the notebook.
- A markdown cell at the end stating what nb15 does *not* show: no human physiological signal, no human perceptual/emotion outcome, no claim that the achieved audio changes would produce the intended arousal shift in a real listener. This is the same sentence as in §1 above — it must appear in the artifact itself, not only in the paper text, because TEI reviewers may watch the video independent of the PDF.

If nb15 delivers all of the above, it is strong enough to be Figure 1 / the supplementary video anchor of the submission. If it ships without the `tier_that_acted` overlay or without the on-screen "SIMULATED" labeling, it will still be a nice demo but will undersell the cascade's actual mechanism and will reopen the "is this just a mood mixtape" critique that the caveats are meant to foreclose.

## 4. Top weaknesses a reviewer will attack, and the cheapest honest mitigation

1. **No human-loop closure at all — the entire "BBCI for emotion regulation" framing is currently unvalidated against any human physiological signal.** This is the biggest and least dodgeable weakness. *Mitigation:* Do not try to hide it — name it as the explicit boundary of the contribution in the title/abstract framing (§1), and cite the iso-principle / tempo-arousal literature to justify why the plant's *sign* (higher tempo/density → higher arousal; matching tempo/energy to reduce a stress-induced elevation) is a reasonable, literature-grounded starting assumption even though it isn't validated here: [Mayr, Starcke, & von Georgi (2021), *Emotion Modulation through Music after Sadness Induction — The Iso Principle in a Controlled Experimental Study*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34886210/) supports the iso-principle mechanism (match current state, then walk toward target — exactly the cascade's design philosophy) and [Watanabe, Kashino, & Ooishi (2017), *Heart Rate Responses Induced by Acoustic Tempo and Its Interaction with Basal Heart Rate*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28266647/) gives a direct, physiological (HR-specific) tempo→arousal sign relationship. Frame these as "why we chose this sign for the toy plant," not as validation of the system.

2. **The toy physiology plant (`dual_lever_plant_step`, `GENRE_EQUILIBRIUM_BAND`) is fabricated and tuned for a "visibly convergent demo," not fit to any real data — so the 6.7×/6.4× numbers characterize the controller against a plant the authors built to be controllable.** A sharp reviewer will note this is close to circular (of course a controller looks great against a plant designed so the controller can succeed). *Mitigation:* State this limitation explicitly and quantitatively — e.g., report that gains/equilibrium bands were hand-tuned, not fit, and reframe the 6.7×/6.4× result as *"a demonstration that the cascade avoids a specific, real failure mode (delay-induced limit-cycling under a naive parallel-controller baseline) rather than a claim about real-world regulation magnitude."* This is the cheapest possible fix — it's a wording change, not new work — and it is honest, because the comparison against the naive-parallel strawman is still a meaningful, mechanism-level result even though the plant is synthetic.

3. **The Latin genre tier rests on N=19 tracks and is explicitly "directional, not precise."** A reviewer will ask why a 3-tier ordering with a shaky top tier is used in headline demos. *Mitigation:* This one is already well-handled in the codebase (`GENRE_ENERGY_ORDER` is a single swappable constant with the caveat written directly into the comment, [`pd_controller.py`](groovegen_project/groovegen/pd_controller.py)) — carry that same honesty into the paper: state the only strongly-supported claim is "R&B is the calmest genre" (N=78, robust) and that the Pop/Latin top-of-scale ordering is a personalizable default, not a universal finding. If nb15's demo walks into the Latin tier, add a caveat annotation at that exact moment (see §3D). Optionally, scope the nb15 hero demo to only cross R&B→Pop (the robust half of the ordering) and relegate the Latin transition to a secondary/appendix clip, which sidesteps the weak evidence in the primary artifact entirely.

4. **Notebook 14's rejection (crossfade) could be read as "the team tried the standard approach and it didn't work" — a reviewer might ask whether the hard-cut fallback is a genuine architectural insight or a workaround that trades one problem (audible seam) for another (audible discontinuity).** *Mitigation:* Turn this into a strength by reporting it as a documented negative result with a principled reason, exactly as `nb14_findings.md` already frames it: crossfading two different-tempo/grid beds fundamentally smears the beat because it asks two incompatible tempo grids to coexist; a downbeat-aligned hard cut avoids that by construction. State plainly in the paper that a hard cut is a deliberate scope-narrowing (accept an audible "edit point," reject an audible "beat smear") rather than an oversight, and that this is a generalizable lesson for anyone building segment-based generative-audio control loops on top of tempo-varying neural beds.

5. **The paper mixes a real empirical finding (danceability/energy, N=122/704) with a pure-simulation control result and an ear-only audio validation — a reviewer could accuse the paper of "evidence laundering" (implying the whole system is validated because one piece of it used real data).** *Mitigation:* Structure the paper (and nb15's own text) so each contribution is explicitly labeled with its evidence tier — e.g., a small table: cascade architecture → simulation only; genre ordering → real survey data (with N caveats); audio transition quality → author's ear (single rater, informal); physiological efficacy → not tested. This kind of self-scoring table is unusual at TEI and would read as unusually rigorous/honest rather than defensive, which works in the paper's favor given the reviewer pool TEI tends to draw (HCI reviewers are generally receptive to explicit epistemics tables).
