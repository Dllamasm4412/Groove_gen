# Experiment 1: Music Perception Rating. IRB support summary

This summary describes the procedure as implemented in software (`experiments/music_rating/`,
experiment version `music-rating-1.0`). It supports the protocol and does not decide the review
category; the institutional IRB decides that.

## Purpose

Participants listen to short computer-generated instrumental music clips and rate each clip on
two separate scales: how much it makes them want to move or dance, and how calming or relaxing it
feels. The ratings check how listeners perceive the fixed music clips themselves.

## What the study does not involve

- No physiological recording of any kind: no PPG, heart rate, or other sensors.
- No haptic (vibration) stimulation.
- No adaptive or feedback-controlled music. Every participant hears the same 27 pre-recorded clips,
  fixed before the study. Only the order differs between participants.
- No vocals or lyrics. The clips are instrumental only.
- No deception.

## Stimuli

- 27 instrumental clips, each 15 to 30 seconds long (9.2 minutes of music in total), plus 2
  practice clips (42 seconds in total) and one sample clip for setting the volume.
- The clips cover a range of styles (calm and ambient, lo-fi, R&B, pop, Latin, hip-hop and
  rock) at different tempos and intensities.
- The clips were generated by computer from symbolic (MIDI) note data, then rendered with a
  standard instrument sound library. They contain no recordings of people.
- Levels are fixed. No clip reaches digital full scale; the loudest peak is about 2 dB below it.

**Listening level.**
- The clips keep the playback levels of the locked GrooveGen system (`pilot-zero-v2c-locked`).
  They are not loudness-normalized, so some clips are louder than others, as they are when the
  system plays them.
- Each participant sets a comfortable listening level once, during setup (step 4).
- Participants are instructed not to change the volume during the experiment.

**What the ratings measure.** The ratings describe each clip as the system plays it, loudness
included. They are not measurements of the composition independent of loudness.

## Procedure

The study runs on a laptop in the lab, through a local web page that works offline. Participants
wear headphones.

1. **Consent.** The researcher completes informed consent with the participant using the
   approved consent form. The researcher then starts the study software and enters a
   participant code. The participant enters no name or other identifying information.
2. **Welcome screen.** The participant confirms by checkbox that they have given consent.
3. **Instructions.** The screen describes the task, both rating questions, and that the
   participant may stop at any time by telling the researcher.
4. **Headphones and volume.** The participant plays a sample clip as often as they like and
   adjusts the volume to a clearly audible, comfortable level. They are asked not to change the
   volume afterwards.
5. **Two practice trials.** These work like the main trials. Their ratings are stored separately
   and are not analysed.
6. **27 main trials, in a random order for each participant.** In each trial:
   - the participant presses Play, and the clip plays once from start to finish;
   - the rating sliders appear only after the clip has ended;
   - the participant answers both questions;
   - the answers are saved, and the next trial begins.
   There is no time limit for answering, and participants may pause between trials.
7. **Completion screen.** The screen thanks the participant and asks them to tell the
   researcher they have finished.

**Expected duration.** About 15 to 20 minutes in total: about 10 minutes of listening, plus time
for instructions, volume setup and rating.

## Rating questions (exact wording)

Each question has its own slider from 0 to 100. The slider shows no starting position: it stays
blank until the participant moves it, and both sliders must be moved before Continue is enabled.
No numbers are displayed.

| Question | Left end (0) | Right end (100) |
|---|---|---|
| How much does this music make you want to move or dance? | Not at all | Extremely |
| How calming or relaxing does this music feel? | Not at all calming | Extremely calming |

The participant does not see the style, tempo or any other description of a clip.

## Risks and discomforts

Risks are expected to be minimal:

- Listening fatigue or mild boredom. The session is short, and participants may pause between
  clips.
- Discomfort from loud sound. The participant sets the volume before the study, the clips have
  fixed levels with headroom, and the participant may lower the volume or stop at any time.
- Some participants may dislike some of the music.

## Voluntary participation and withdrawal

Participants may stop at any time, for any reason, by telling the researcher. Stopping has no
penalty.

**What the software does when someone stops.**
- The software saves each answer as soon as it is given, so a stopped session holds the trials
  completed so far.
- The researcher can record a withdrawal with the `--withdraw` option, optionally adding a note.
  This marks the session as withdrawn and prevents it from being resumed.
- The software never deletes or changes saved answers on its own.

**Not yet decided.** Whether a withdrawn participant's data are kept, excluded from analysis or
deleted will follow the final consent form and protocol. The researcher carries out that decision
manually.

## Data collected

**For each trial:**
- the participant code;
- an internal clip identifier;
- the trial position;
- the two ratings (0 to 100);
- timestamps for when the clip started, when it ended, and when the response was given;
- the response time;
- how many times the clip was started (more than once only if the page was reloaded during
  playback).

**For each session:**
- start and end times and completion status;
- the randomization seed;
- software and stimulus-set versions;
- a log of interface events (for example, page reloads and audio pauses).

No names, contact details, audio or video recordings, or physiological data are collected by the
software.

## Confidentiality and data handling

- Data are stored only on the study laptop. The study page is served from the laptop itself
  (`127.0.0.1`), does not need the Internet, and sends no data elsewhere.
- Files are labelled with the participant code only. Any link between codes and identities is
  kept separately, under the protocol's data-management plan.
- Participant data are excluded from the code repository by configuration.
- Retention, backup, and access arrangements follow the approved protocol.
