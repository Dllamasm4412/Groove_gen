# Experiment 1, remote version: Music Perception Rating. IRB support summary

This summary describes the remote procedure as implemented in Qualtrics
(`experiments/music_rating/qualtrics/`, version `music-rating-remote-1.0`). It supports the
protocol and does not decide the review category; the institutional IRB decides that.

An in-lab version of the same task also exists; it is described in `../IRB_SUMMARY.md`.

## Purpose

Participants listen to short computer-generated instrumental music clips and rate each clip on
two separate scales: how much it makes them want to move or dance, and how calming or relaxing it
feels. The ratings check how listeners perceive the fixed music clips themselves.

## What the study does not involve

- No physiological recording: no heart rate, PPG or other sensors.
- No haptic (vibration) stimulation.
- No adaptive or feedback-controlled music. Everyone hears the same pre-recorded clips; only the
  order differs between participants.
- No vocals or lyrics. The clips are instrumental only.
- No deception.
- No audio or video recording of participants.

## Setting and delivery

- Participants take part remotely, on their own laptop or desktop computer with headphones.
- They use a web survey hosted on the university's Qualtrics account.
- [Recruitment method and compensation: to be completed by the researcher.]

## Stimuli

- 27 instrumental clips, each about 8 to 16 seconds long (4.7 minutes of music in total).
- Each clip is the first four bars of a piece generated by the locked GrooveGen system
  (`pilot-zero-v2c-locked`) from symbolic (MIDI) note data and rendered with a standard
  instrument sound library. The clips contain no recordings of people.
- 2 practice clips and one volume-setting sample.
- The sound files are served from the Qualtrics file library or a public web folder, under
  anonymous file names. They contain no participant data.

**Listening level.**
- The clips keep the playback levels of the locked GrooveGen system; they are not
  loudness-normalized. No clip reaches digital full scale.
- Each participant sets a comfortable listening level once, during setup.
- Participants are asked not to change the volume during the survey. Afterwards they are asked
  whether they did.

**What the ratings measure.** The ratings describe each clip as the system plays it, loudness
included. They are not measurements of the composition independent of loudness.

## Procedure

1. **Consent.** The participant reads the online consent information and chooses "I agree" to
   continue, or "I do not agree" to leave.
2. **Device and setting.** The instructions ask the participant to use a laptop or desktop
   computer with headphones, in a quiet place. The survey records the browser type and screen
   size.
3. **Instructions.** The screen describes the task, both rating questions, the break, and that
   the participant may stop at any time by closing the page.
4. **Volume setup.** The participant plays a sample clip as often as they like and sets a
   comfortable volume.
5. **Two practice trials,** which work like the main trials and are not analysed.
6. **27 main trials, in a random order for each participant.** In each trial:
   - the participant presses Play, and the clip plays once from start to finish (if playback is
     interrupted, the clip restarts from the beginning);
   - the rating sliders appear only after the clip has ended;
   - the participant answers both questions.

   There is no time limit for answering. After the 14th trial, a break screen invites the
   participant to rest.
7. **Final questions:**
   - one attention item ("please move this slider all the way to the right");
   - whether headphones were used throughout;
   - whether the volume was changed;
   - whether the setting was quiet;
   - an optional question about technical problems.
8. **Completion screen.**

Headphone use is requested in the instructions and checked by self-report at the end. There is no
automated headphone test.

**Expected duration.** About 10 to 15 minutes.

## Rating questions (exact wording)

Each question has its own slider, recorded from 0 to 100.
- The slider shows no starting position; both sliders must be moved before the participant can
  continue.
- Six evenly spaced, unlabeled tick marks run along each slider.
- No numbers are displayed.

| Question | Left end (0) | Right end (100) |
|---|---|---|
| How much does this music make you want to move or dance? | Not at all | Extremely |
| How calming or relaxing does this music feel? | Not at all calming | Extremely calming |

The participant does not see the style, tempo or any other description of a clip.

## Risks and discomforts

Risks are expected to be minimal:
- Listening fatigue or mild boredom. The session is short and includes a break.
- Discomfort from loud sound. Participants set their own volume before the survey and may lower
  it or stop at any time.
- Some participants may dislike some of the music.

## Voluntary participation and withdrawal

Participants may stop at any time by closing the survey, without penalty.

- Qualtrics keeps partially completed responses according to the survey's settings.

## Data collected

**For each trial:**
- an internal clip identifier;
- the trial position;
- the two ratings (0 to 100);
- the time taken to rate;
- whether the clip played in full, and how many times it was started.

**For each participant:**
- consent response;
- attention item;
- post-survey answers;
- browser type and screen size;
- survey start and end times and completion status;
- [a recruitment-platform ID, if used].

**Not collected:** names, contact details, audio or video recordings, or physiological data.

[Qualtrics may record IP address and approximate location by default. Turn on Qualtrics'
"Anonymize responses" option if the protocol requires it.]

## Confidentiality and data handling

- Responses are stored on the university's licensed Qualtrics account.
- The researcher downloads them for analysis to a study computer, where data files are kept out
  of the code repository.
- Records carry the recruitment ID or a Qualtrics response ID, not names.
- Retention, backup and access follow the approved protocol.
